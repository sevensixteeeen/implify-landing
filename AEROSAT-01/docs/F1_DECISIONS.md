## 1. What F1 is

**F1: barometer/accelerometer disagreement.** A sensor can deliver values that are finite and in range but wrong (bias step, drift, scale error). Sample rejection (saturated or non-finite, TELEMETRY_PROTOCOL section 3.4) does not catch this. The estimator then follows the wrong sensor, which can produce a false APOGEE and a premature drag-device deployment.

F1 asks for a detector that notices when the two sensors disagree by more than their noise explains, and a defined avionics response.

## 6b. Item 2: definitions

Symbols, with units. `k` indexes valid barometer samples (50 Hz, 20 ms apart). REF-01 sensor values are quoted from `scenarios/ref-01.json`; they are inputs, not results.

| Symbol | Unit | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| `h_b,k` | m | Barometric altitude above the pad from sample `k`, by the avionics pressure law: `h = (T_ref/L)(1 - (p/p_ref)^(R L/g0))` (`estimator.py`). |
| `sigma_p` | Pa | Per-sample barometer noise including quantisation: `sigma_p = sqrt(sigma_n^2 + q^2/12)`. REF-01: `sigma_n = 3.0 Pa`, `q = 1.0 Pa`. |
| `dh/dp` | m/Pa | Slope of the pressure law at the measured pressure: `(T_ref/L)(R L/g0)(p/p_ref)^(R L/g0 - 1)/p_ref`, taken as a magnitude. |
| `sigma_h,k` | m | Barometric altitude noise of sample `k`: `sigma_p * dh/dp(p_k)`. Grows as air density falls. |
| `r_k` | m | **Filter innovation**: `h_b,k` minus the filter altitude after the prediction step and before the correction (`residual_m` in `ComplementaryFilter.correct`). |
| `q_I,k` | 1 | Normalised innovation: `abs(r_k) / sigma_h,k`. Its expected spread `s_I` (in units of `sigma_h`) follows from the steady-state covariance of the fixed-gain filter (gains `K_h`, `K_v`, 20 ms correction period, four 5 ms predictions driven by accelerometer noise). `s_I` is to be computed by the audit, not assumed. |
| `h_DR(t)`, `v_DR(t)` | m, m/s | Accelerometer-only dead reckoning. Starts at the end of calibration with `h = v = 0`. Every 5 ms: `a = (f_z - b_a) - g0`, `h += v dt + a dt^2/2`, `v += a dt`, the last valid acceleration is held for at most 100 ms; after that the dead reckoning is invalid for the rest of the flight. It never uses the barometer. |
| `D_k` | m | `h_b,k - h_DR(t_k)`. |
| `dD_k` | m | Mean of `D` over samples `k .. k-4`, minus mean of `D` over samples `k-10 .. k-14`. Block centres are 200 ms apart. Needs 15 contiguous valid samples (20 ms spacing) and valid dead reckoning. |
| `dz_k` | 1 | **Historical statistic, superseded:** `abs(dD_k) / sigma_h,k`. Its denominator is not the spread of `dD` (see below). |
| `q_D,k` | 1 | **Corrected disagreement statistic** (below). Not called a z-score: it is not shown to be unit-normal. |

**Derivation of the `sqrt(2/5)` factor.** In the sensor model each barometer sample carries independent noise of spread `sigma_h` (the bias is constant and cancels in a difference). The mean of 5 independent samples has spread `sigma_h / sqrt(5)`. The two blocks in `dD` share no samples, so their noise is independent and the variances add: `Var(dD) = 2 * sigma_h^2 / 5`, spread `sqrt(2/5) * sigma_h`. Dividing `dD` by `sigma_h` alone (`dz`) therefore yields a statistic whose noise spread is `sqrt(2/5)`, not 1.

**Other contributions to `dD`, to be measured by the audit:**

- Dead reckoning: velocity error spread `sigma_v,DR(t)^2 = (sigma_a t / sqrt(N_cal))^2 + sigma_a^2 dt t` (calibration bias error and integrated accelerometer noise; `N_cal` accelerometer samples in calibration, `t` time since calibration). It contributes `sigma_DR = 0.2 s * sigma_v,DR(t)`.
- Altitude scale error: an error `dT` in the reference temperature scales barometric altitude by `kappa = dT/T_ref`, so over 0.2 s it adds about `kappa * abs(v) * 0.2 s`. This grows with speed and is the reason for F1-D3.
- Held acceleration (up to 100 ms) is not modelled; the audit reports it if it occurs.
- Successive `dD_k` overlap and are correlated; this affects counts of consecutive alarms, not the per-sample spread.

**Corrected statistic:**

`q_D,k = abs(dD_k) / sqrt( (2/5) sigma_h,k^2 + sigma_DR(t_k)^2 + (kappa * abs(v_hat,k) * 0.2 s)^2 )`

with `v_hat` the estimated vertical speed (avionics data only, FR-AVI-01). An alarm threshold on `q_D` is equivalent to a threshold on `abs(dD)` that grows with estimated speed, as F1-D3 requires. `kappa` has to become an avionics configuration value; its value is an open decision.

### 6e.1 Policy B: the disagreement detector

**Statistic.** `q_D,k` from section 6b, evaluated at every valid barometer sample `k` for which `dD_k` is defined:

`q_D,k = abs(dD_k) / S_k`, with `S_k = sqrt( (2/5) sigma_h,k^2 + sigma_DR(t_k)^2 + (kappa * abs(v_hat,k) * 0.2 s)^2 )`

All inputs are avionics data (FR-AVI-01): the barometer sample, an accelerometer-only dead reckoning (a new avionics component, section 6b), the estimator's vertical speed `v_hat`, and configuration values `sigma_p`, `sigma_a`, `kappa`. `N_cal` and `t` come from the avionics' own calibration and tick.

**How speed enters (F1-D3).** Only through the `kappa * abs(v_hat) * 0.2 s` term. A flag needs `abs(dD) > T * S_k`, so the allowed disagreement in metres grows with estimated speed. The detector is never switched off.

**Threshold, from the noise model.**

1. Noise part: `dD = b + n`. `n` is the barometer and dead-reckoning noise, close to Gaussian (a difference of two means of 5 independent samples), with spread `S_n = sqrt((2/5) sigma_h^2 + sigma_DR^2)`. `b` is the altitude-scale bias, bounded by `abs(b) <= kappa * abs(v) * 0.2 s` when `kappa` bounds the scale error (6e.5).
2. Because `S >= S_n` and `S >= abs(b)`: `q_D <= 1 + abs(n)/S_n`. So per sample, `P(q_D > T) <= 2 Q(T - 1)`, with `Q` the standard normal tail.
3. Samples per flight: the detector runs from the first defined `dD` (calibration end + 280 ms) to the deployment command. The latest possible command in any audit scenario is the liftoff timer (6e.4), at most about 35 s. Bound: `N <= 50 Hz * 40 s = 2,000` samples.
4. Per-flight false-flag bound, ignoring persistence and correlation (both only lower it): `P_flight <= N * 2 Q(T - 1)`.
5. Budget (for approval): `P_flight <= 1e-5`. This needs `2 Q(T - 1) <= 5e-9`, so `T - 1 >= 5.84`.

**Proposed threshold: `T = 7.0`.** Then `2 Q(6.0) = 1.97e-9` per sample and `P_flight <= 3.9e-6`. Over the 1,206 healthy flights of the audit set the expected number of false flags is at most about 0.005 under this model. That is a prediction from the model, not a result.

**Validity of the bound.** Step 2 needs `abs(b) <= kappa * abs(v) * 0.2 s`. The bound therefore holds only while the sensor biases are within the 3-sigma assumptions behind `kappa` (6e.5): thermometer bias within `3 * bias_sigma_k`, barometer bias within `3 * bias_sigma_pa`, and flight altitude within the `h_max` used for the geopotential term. Outside those, the bound says nothing.

**Persistence: 3 consecutive defined samples above `T` raise the flag.**

- Successive `dD_k` overlap, so they are correlated. Derived from the block structure: correlation `(5 - j)/5` at lag `j = 1..4`, zero at 5, negative from 6 to 14. Persistence is therefore **not** counted in the false-flag bound above.
- It is kept as a guard against a single bad value inside the `dD` computation (for example one held accelerometer value), at the cost of 40 ms.
- It cannot hide a barometer fault. Any change in one barometer sample moves 5 consecutive `dD` values (the block length), so a fault visible to a single-sample rule is also visible to a 3-sample rule.
- Latency is not critical. The earliest false APOGEE is 45 ms after the first faulted barometer sample (10-tick debounce at 5 ms). The normal-path command follows 1,000 ms later (`deploy_delay_after_apogee_ms`). With the flag latched (6e.2), the flag must be raised within about 1,045 ms of the fault onset, not within 45 ms.

**Availability.**

| Condition | Statistic | Flag |
|---|---|---|
| Fewer than 15 contiguous valid barometer samples (start of flight, or within 280 ms after a rejected barometer sample) | undefined | no change. Barometer rejection is already handled by the existing rules; barometric confirmation needs valid samples. |
| Dead reckoning invalid (accelerometer invalid for more than 100 ms, section 6b) | undefined | **raised, reason `UNCHECKABLE`** (proposed; needs approval) |

*Why `UNCHECKABLE` raises the flag:* all 180 baseline ascent deployments through barometric confirmation are accelerometer-invalid plus barometer-drift runs (6d). In those runs `dD` cannot be computed, so a detector that stays silent when it cannot check would leave all 180 in place, against F1-D2. *Cost:* any flight that loses the accelerometer after liftoff deploys only by a backup timer.

**Detection floor (predicted, analytic).** A disagreement is flagged when `abs(dD) > 7.0 * S`. With `sigma_h = sigma_p * dh/dp` and `dh/dp ~ 1/(rho g)`:

| Where | `sigma_h` | Floor on `abs(dD)` at low speed | Equivalent drift rate (dD over 0.2 s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| REF-01 pad | about 0.25 m | about 1.1 m | about 5.6 m/s |
| REF-01 apogee (2,126 m) | about 0.31 m | about 1.4 m | about 6.8 m/s |
| `high8k` apogee (10,578 m MSL) | about 0.80 m | about 3.5 m | about 18 m/s |

Prediction to check in item 8:
- **At risk:** the `high8k` drift -20 m/s case. Its disagreement is close to the floor there.
- **Expected not to flag:** the 0-ascent baseline cases below the floor (drift -2 and -5 m/s, single-sample ±5 m spikes) stay unflagged. That is harmless, because none of them deployed during ascent in the baseline.

### 6e.2 Flag lifetime: latched for the rest of the flight

**Rule.** Once raised, the disagreement flag stays raised until the end of the flight. Recorded: first raise tick, reason (`DISAGREEMENT` or `UNCHECKABLE`), and `q_D` at that tick. Agreement afterwards does not clear it.

**Evidence (6c recovery, spike and false-peak cases, baseline 6d).** In every one of these runs that deployed during ascent, the fault had already ended when the command was issued:

| Cases | Ascent runs | Command after the fault ended |
|---|---|---|
| Step -100 m at mid for 40, 60, 100, 300 ms | 100 | 745-1,005 ms |
| Step -100 m at mid for 1000 ms | 30 | 45-65 ms |
| Drift -50 m/s at mid for 1000 ms | 10 | 525-545 ms |
| Step -30 m at near for 40, 100, 300 ms | 90 | 745-1,005 ms |
| Single +5000 Pa sample at mid and at `A - 2 s` | 80 | 1,044 ms |
| False peak +60 m 60 ms, +150 m 200 ms, +500 m 1 s | 120 | 1,045-1,265 ms |

`dD` stops showing a fault at most 280 ms after its last faulted sample, when that sample leaves the older block. A flag that clears once the sensors agree again would therefore already have cleared in **400 of these 430 runs** (all except the 30 step runs lasting 1000 ms) before the command, and the normal path would have deployed during ascent as in the baseline. The cause is that the fault leaves a latched false APOGEE behind; agreement afterwards does not undo it. Hence the flag must latch.

**Cost.** A harmless transient that raises the flag (predicted: the +5000 Pa spike during boost, which did not deploy during ascent in the baseline) moves deployment to a backup timer: about 2 s after true apogee instead of about 1 s (6e.4 table).

**Quality.** The estimate is `UNRELIABLE` from the flag tick to the end of the flight (F1-D4). Events detected later take that quality (`APOGEE`, `TOUCHDOWN`). Events already recorded are never rewritten.

### 6e.3 F1-D4 deployment restriction

**Evaluation order inside `FlightComputer.tick`** (changes the current order, where quality is computed before the prediction step):

1. Classify samples.
2. Predict and correct the filter.
3. Advance the dead reckoning.
4. Evaluate `q_D` and update the flag.
5. Compute estimate quality, including the flag.
6. Call `FlightStateMachine.update` with the flag.

A flag raised on a tick therefore applies to the deployment decision on that same tick.

**Rules in `_decide_deployment`:**

| Situation | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Flag not raised | Unchanged: normal path, then barometric confirmation, then burnout timer, as today. Existing EVT-19 behaviour is unaffected. |
| Flag raised | Only a backup timer may deploy (burnout timer, or liftoff timer of 6e.4). The normal path and barometric confirmation are not evaluated. |
| Reliable APOGEE already recorded when the flag is raised, command not yet issued | The reliable APOGEE stays recorded as it was (never rewritten), but it no longer authorises deployment. **Timer restored:** today a reliable apogee "cancels" the timer only because the reliable branch returns before the timer is checked (`state_machine.py`, reliable branch). Under the flag that branch is skipped, so `_timer_expired` is evaluated on every tick in DESCENT. No timer state is reset; `_timer_fired` keeps its meaning. |
| Flag raised in COAST before any APOGEE | APOGEE is still detected; it is recorded `UNRELIABLE` (6e.2). The burnout timer's inferred-apogee rule in COAST is unchanged. |
| Flag raised after the command | No effect on deployment. |
| Burnout timer and liftoff timer expire on the same tick | Burnout timer branch first; `_deployed` latches, so there is one command. |

**Deployment event when restricted.** Source `BACKUP_TIMER`, quality `UNRELIABLE`, reason naming the timer, the flag tick and the flag reason.

**Exactly one command:** unchanged mechanism. Every path goes through `_deploy`, which latches `_deployed`.

### 6e.4 F1-D6 liftoff timer

**Formula.** `L = t_burn + D_B`, where:

- `t_burn` is the end of the thrust table (`vehicle.thrust_curve`, last `t_ms`, measured from ignition).
- `D_B` is `avionics.backup_deployment_delay_ms`.
- The timer starts at detected LIFTOFF and expires at `t_LO,det + L`.
- `L` is derived by the scenario loader, with no new configuration field. There is precedent: `load_avionics_config` already reads `timing.t_ignition_ms` and `sensors.baro.rate_hz` to validate the avionics configuration.

**Derivation.**

- *No deployment during ascent from this timer:* expiry minus true apogee `= (t_LO,det - t_ign) + (D_B - C_true)`, where `C_true` is the true coast from burnout to apogee.
  - `t_LO,det >= t_ign`: liftoff detection needs specific force above `liftoff_specific_force_mps2`, which needs thrust.
  - `D_B > C`: existing load check against `expected_coast_duration_ms`.
  - So expiry is after true apogee whenever `C >= C_true`. That is the same assumption the burnout timer already relies on.
- *No interference with a healthy flight:* the normal command comes at detected apogee + `deploy_delay_after_apogee_ms`. The liftoff timer comes at least `D_B - C_true` after true apogee, so it never acts first when `D_B - C_true > deploy_delay + apogee detection latency`.

**Values** (truth figures from a sensor-free truth run of each scenario; "liftoff criterion met" is when the true specific force first meets the avionics liftoff rule, 10 samples above threshold):

| Scenario | `t_burn` (ms) | `D_B` (ms) | `L` (ms) | `D_B - C` (ms) | Liftoff criterion met | Predicted expiry vs true apogee |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ref01` (REF-01) | 3,200 | 20,000 | **23,200** | 1,953 | 3,065 | +2,018 ms |
| `zero_drag` | 3,200 | 26,000 | **29,200** | 2,335 | 3,065 | +2,400 ms |
| `low_thrust` | 16,200 | 11,000 | **27,200** | 2,044 | 7,675 | +6,719 ms (late: liftoff is detected about 4.6 s after ignition at 280 N) |
| `high4k` | 3,200 | 21,477 | **24,677** | 2,000 | 3,065 | +2,065 ms |
| `high8k` | 3,200 | 22,763 | **25,963** | 2,000 | 3,065 | +2,065 ms |

**On expiry, if nothing has deployed:**
- Command deployment: source `BACKUP_TIMER`, quality `UNRELIABLE`, reason "liftoff-anchored backup timer".
- If no APOGEE is recorded, record an inferred APOGEE (`BACKUP_TIMER`, `UNRELIABLE`) and enter DESCENT, as the burnout timer already does in COAST. From BOOST this skips BURNOUT, which stays unrecorded.
- Allowed while the flag is raised (it is a backup timer under F1-D4). Never fires if anything has already deployed.

**Out of scope:** LIFTOFF never detected (F1-D6).

### 6e.5 `kappa`: value and basis

`kappa` bounds the relative error between the avionics barometric altitude and true geometric altitude. There are three terms, added linearly (conservative):

1. **Reference temperature:** `3 * sigma_T / T_ref`. `sigma_T` is the thermometer bias spread (REF-01 sensor configuration: 0.5 K). The 1 s calibration mean removes noise, not bias. `T_ref` is the pad temperature.
2. **Geopotential vs geometric altitude:** the avionics pressure law assumes constant `g0`; truth uses inverse-square gravity and geometric altitude. Their ratio is about `(1 + h/R0)^2`, so the bound is `2 h_max / R0`, with `h_max` the highest MSL altitude of the flight and `R0 = 6,356,766 m`.
3. **Barometer bias:** a constant bias `delta = 3 * bias_sigma_pa` (REF-01: 90 Pa) enters both the pad reference `p_ref` and every reading. Through the avionics pressure law the local altitude slope changes by the factor `((p + delta)/p)^(n-1) * (p_ref/(p_ref + delta))^n`, with `n = R L / g0`. The term is the largest deviation of that factor from 1 over the flight. It is largest at apogee, where `p` is lowest. Evaluated for both signs of `delta`, at the true pad and minimum pressures of a sensor-free truth run.

| Scenario | `T_ref` (K) | `h_max` MSL (m) | Temperature term | Geopotential term | Barometer-bias term (at apogee) | `kappa` | **Configuration value** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ref01` (REF-01) | 288.15 | 2,126 | 0.00521 | 0.00067 | 0.00110 | 0.00698 | **0.007** |
| `zero_drag` | 288.15 | 3,102 | 0.00521 | 0.00098 | 0.00122 | 0.00740 | **0.008** |
| `low_thrust` | 288.15 | 1,096 | 0.00521 | 0.00034 | 0.00099 | 0.00654 | **0.007** |
| `high4k` | 262.15 | 6,360 | 0.00572 | 0.00200 | 0.00190 | 0.00963 | **0.010** |
| `high8k` | 236.15 | 10,578 | 0.00635 | 0.00333 | 0.00350 | 0.01318 | **0.014** |

Values are rounded up to the next 0.001. The barometer-bias term agrees with the project owner's check (0.0011, 0.0019, 0.0035 for `ref01`, `high4k`, `high8k`).

**Configuration values.** `sigma_p`, `sigma_a` and `kappa` are avionics configuration values, written by the scenario author with the formulas above:

| Field (`avionics` section) | Formula | REF-01 value | Audit scenarios |
|---|---|---|---|
| `detector_sigma_p_pa` | `sqrt(noise_sigma_pa^2 + resolution_pa^2 / 12)` = `sqrt(3.0^2 + 1.0^2/12)` = 3.0139, rounded up | **3.014** | 3.014 (same barometer) |
| `detector_sigma_a_mps2` | `sqrt(noise_sigma_mps2^2 + resolution_mps2^2 / 12)` = `sqrt(0.05^2 + 0.005^2/12)` = 0.05002, rounded up | **0.0501** | 0.0501 (same accelerometer) |
| `detector_kappa` | table above | **0.007** | `zero_drag` 0.008, `low_thrust` 0.007, `high4k` 0.010, `high8k` 0.014 |

The avionics never reads the scenario's `sensors` section for these. It only sees its own configuration (FR-AVI-01). The liftoff timer (6e.4) is derived from the `vehicle` section and the existing backup delay, not from the sensors.
